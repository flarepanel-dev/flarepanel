# Security

We welcome reports of security issues in FlarePanel.

## Report a vulnerability

Email **security@flarepanel.dev**. Please don't report security issues in public GitHub issues, pull requests or community channels. The same contact is in our [security.txt](https://flarepanel.dev/.well-known/security.txt).

To help us triage quickly, include what you can:

- the affected component (API, dashboard, a tracking SDK) and the endpoint or file involved
- the version or commit you tested, or "flarepanel.dev"
- what the issue is and its impact
- steps to reproduce, or a proof of concept
- a suggested fix, if you have one

## What to expect

- We try to acknowledge reports within 3 business days.
- We aim to confirm the issue and send an assessment within 7 days, then keep you posted while we fix it.
- When the fix ships, we tell you so you can confirm it.

## Coordinated disclosure

Please give us a reasonable chance to release a fix and notify self-hosters before any public write-up. If you plan to publish, a short note on timing helps us make sure the patch is live for everyone first.

## Scope

In scope: the FlarePanel code (API, dashboard, the official SDKs) and the hosted service at flarepanel.dev, dashboard.flarepanel.dev and api.flarepanel.dev. For a self-hosted install, please confirm the issue on the latest version first.

Out of scope, unless you can show concrete, exploitable impact:

- denial-of-service or resource-exhaustion attacks
- social engineering, phishing or physical attacks
- automated scanner output with no demonstrated impact
- missing best-practice headers without a working exploit
- vulnerabilities in third-party dependencies without a proof of concept against FlarePanel
- issues that need a compromised device or a man-in-the-middle position on the victim's own network
- self-XSS, and clickjacking on pages with no sensitive actions

## Safe harbor

We treat security research done in good faith under this policy as authorized and won't pursue legal action over accidental, good-faith violations. While you research:

- only touch accounts and data you own or may test; never access, change or delete other users' data
- use test accounts and avoid anything that degrades the service for others (no denial of service, spam or high-volume automated testing)
- stop and report once you've confirmed an issue, and keep the details private until a fix ships
